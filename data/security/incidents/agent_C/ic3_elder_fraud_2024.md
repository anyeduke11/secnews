# FBI IC3 2024 Elder Fraud Report — Key Findings (excerpt)

**Scope**: The IC3 received 147,127 complaints from subjects over the age of 60 in 2024, with losses exceeding $4.8 billion — a 43 percent increase in losses over 2023.

## Tech Support & Government Impersonation Scams

Callers pose as bank fraud investigators, federal agents, or tech support staff, instructing victims that their accounts are compromised and funds must be moved to a "safe government account" via wire transfer, cash couriers, or cryptocurrency ATMs. The average loss per incident exceeded $55,000.

## Romance Scams

Perpetrators build trust over weeks through dating platforms, then introduce an "investment opportunity," typically cryptocurrency trading on spoofed platforms. Victims see fabricated dashboards showing gains before being asked to pay fabricated "taxes" or "withdrawal fees."

## Account Takeover (ATO)

Elderly victims report unauthorized changes to their banking credentials after responding to phishing texts mimicking fraud alerts from their financial institution.

**Recommendation**: Financial institutions should flag unusual wire instructions to "government safe accounts," educate tellers on elder coercion indicators, and hold large third-party transfers for verification.
