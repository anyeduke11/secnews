# INTERPOL Financial Crimes — Fraud Typologies (excerpt)

## Business Email Compromise and Impersonation Fraud

Organized crime groups research target companies and executives, register look-alike domains, and insert themselves into ongoing correspondence to redirect invoice payments to mule accounts. Combined losses reported by member countries grew steadily year over year.

## Money Mule Networks

Fraud proceeds are laundered through networks of recruited or unwitting money mules who open bank accounts in their own names. Students and job seekers are targeted through "work from home" advertisements. Financial institutions detect mule activity through rapid in-and-out transfer patterns and shared device or IP fingerprints across accounts.

## Vishing and Smishing Against Bank Customers

Callers spoofing bank hotlines pressure victims into disclosing one-time passwords or approving push notifications, enabling immediate account takeover. SMS phishing lures mimic parcel delivery and tax refund notices.

**Recommended countermeasures**: banks should enforce cooling-off periods for credential resets, detect simultaneous logins from distant geolocations, and participate in rapid fund-freeze cooperation channels with counterpart institutions.
